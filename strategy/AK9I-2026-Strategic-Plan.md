**American K-9 Interdiction, LLC (AK9I)**

> **Purpose and provenance:** This document revises the June 2024 AK9I Strategic Plan (v3.2), incorporates Paul Roushia's handwritten 2026 status annotations and subsequent AK9I leadership decisions, and aligns with the AK9I Operating System's canonical seven-stage customer pipeline. Reported completion, approved strategic direction, draft recommendations, and unverified claims are distinguished rather than silently conflated.

## Executive Summary

American K-9 Interdiction is a working-K9 education and services company whose mission is to prepare capable working-dog handlers and trainers, deliver professional K9 operations, and support lasting student and customer success.

The 2024 plan placed considerable emphasis on enrollment growth, certification expansion, major facilities changes, new contracts, and establishing an internal breeding program. The 2026 update retains the underlying educational and operational ambitions while changing the order of execution: **first stabilize finances and execution; then validate program and contract economics; then scale what produces repeatable, verified outcomes.**

### Five 2026 strategic priorities

1. **Financial resilience and management accountability:** departmental budgets, spending authorization, 13-week cash forecasting, reliable financial reporting, defined responsibilities, and documented actions.
2. **Educational excellence:** catalog-aligned **7:1** student-to-instructor planning, financially viable 5-/8-/12-week course delivery, student support, accurate instructional records, graduate completion, and employment outcomes.
3. **Seven-stage customer lifecycle:** Salesforce used consistently from Awareness through Advocacy, with explicit communications, owners, data standards, and handoffs for students and contract clients.
4. **Profitable, credible contract operations:** government, school-district, law-enforcement, and qualified private-entity opportunities supported by canine readiness, certified handlers, delivery controls, invoicing, renewal, and measurable contract contribution.
5. **Disciplined longer-term options:** a stage-gated national certification-standard initiative, a sale-preferred decision on Zuni with an evidence-based contract exception, and deferral of a capital-intensive canine breeding program.

### Leadership decisions and status boundaries

- **Ratio:** 7:1 replaces the previous student-to-instructor planning target throughout the current objectives. The published Student Catalog and program-specific supervisory requirements govern final approved implementation.
- **Salesforce:** already implemented as AK9I's CRM, but detailed lifecycle, communications, data hygiene, and analytics require completion and validation.
- **Enrollment:** Paul's reported fourth-quarter enrollment reduction of approximately 50% and an approximate 7–10 monthly student baseline need reconciled supporting records. **Economic downturn is not an established causal finding.**
- **Certification:** Objective 8 is retained as exploratory work. AK9I has reported discussions with staff in **U.S. Representative Jen Kiggans's office**, with little forward movement in the current 2026 election cycle. The conversations do not constitute an endorsement, recognition, or approved certification scheme. Objective 11 is not retained as a duplicate standalone launch initiative.
- **Zuni:** leadership has agreed that **selling Zuni is the preferred direction to strengthen AK9I's financial position**. Retention is a conditional alternative only if near-term contracts come to fruition and demonstrably justify long-term property needs and costs.
- **Breeding:** former Objective 12 is deferred as a funded initiative, with a comprehensive capital allocation, operational readiness, canine welfare, and economic rationale.
- **Historical amounts:** the 2024 estimates do not represent authorized 2026 budgets and must be replaced with current validated financials.

### Organization of this plan

Education Objectives **1, 2, 3A, 4, 5, 6, and 3B** and Business Objectives **7, 8, 9, and 10** retain their historical identifiers to make reconciliation practical. Finance, organizational accountability, regulatory readiness, and data governance are mandatory cross-cutting controls. Former Objectives **11 and 12** are documented in a separate disposition section rather than presented as funded active projects.

No numerical growth target, official credential claim, capital investment, or contract opportunity is treated as achieved without an identified evidence source.

## 1. Management Operating Framework

### 1.1 Three coordinated systems

The operating model consists of:

- **Management Operating System:** functional owners, personnel authority, role clarity, meeting cadence, budgets, scorecards, actions, and evidence of execution.
- **AK9I Brand System:** recognizable identity, approved claims, professional behavior, consistent content, and quality standards across staff and vendors.
- **Customer Pipeline:** one seven-stage architecture applied to student and contract-client relationships with appropriate lane-specific handoffs.

These management systems supplement, rather than replace, approved academic programs, student-record controls, legal requirements, and accreditation obligations.

### 1.2 Cross-cutting goal — financial management and stabilization

**Goal:** Establish reliable cash visibility and financial controls and fund only those activities that AK9I can deliver safely and sustainably.

**Strategies**

1. Prepare a **13-week rolling cash forecast** linked to current bank balances, receivables, payroll, vendor obligations, debt, and known commitments.
2. Establish monthly functional budgets for Education/Training, Student Services/Administration, Kennel/Canine Operations, Contracts/Field Operations, Business Development/Marketing, Facilities, Technology, and HR/Finance. Reconcile functional categories to the existing accounting chart of accounts.
3. Implement written leadership-approved expenditure authorizations with a temporarily restrictive spending posture, explicit delegated limits, and a documented emergency safety/canine-welfare process.
4. Standardize order, authorization, receipt, vendor payment, payroll, tuition/payment links, invoices, and reconciliation; maintain appropriate separation of duties.
5. Compare actual versus budget and report aging receivables, unbilled services, student revenue, dog procurement, instructor utilization, course contribution, contract contribution, and net cash position.
6. Require a formal business case and financing plan for significant facilities, staffing, technology, or service-line investments.
7. Document critical finance and HR knowledge during leadership transitions and identify a primary operator and backup reviewer for each recurring process.

**Evaluation:** forecast variance, cash runway, budget variance, unapproved spending, invoice age, reconciliations completed, payroll accuracy, course contribution, and contract contribution.

### 1.3 Cross-cutting goal — organizational accountability

**Goal:** Define who owns each activity and who has final decision authority.

**Strategies**

1. Approve separate views for **functional work allocation** and **personnel reporting hierarchy**.
2. Update titles, job descriptions, personnel documents, employment letters, authorities, and systems access when responsibilities change.
3. Preserve sufficient protected administrative and financial capacity while maintaining adequate animal-care coverage and cross-training.
4. Use weekly operational meetings, monthly financial reviews, quarterly strategic reviews, and a consistent action register with one owner, due date, next action, blocker, and evidence.
5. Adopt written handoff and escalation procedures between Business Development, Student Services, Training, Kennels, Field Operations, Facilities, Data/Systems, and Finance.

**Evaluation:** approved role coverage, on-time actions, audit-ready personnel records, unresolved handoffs, and department scorecard completeness.

### 1.4 Cross-cutting goal — compliance and quality assurance

**Goal:** Maintain institutional authorization, veteran-benefit accuracy, reliable academic records, and lawful, professional K9 operations.

**Strategies**

1. Assign owners and backups for SCHEV, applicable accreditation obligations, VA/SAA, DCJS, academic records, licensing, employment verification, and contract requirements.
2. Maintain a controlled regulatory calendar, evidence repository, internal review schedule, corrective-action register, and leadership escalation.
3. Verify all necessary approvals before materially revised academic offerings become operational.
4. Maintain student benefit records, enrollment/termination records, course outcomes, placement evidence, and appropriate alumni attestations.
5. Maintain canine health and training records, handler credentials, custody controls where required, deployment documentation, and incident evidence.
6. Evaluate any long-range Title IV pathway independently of current veteran-benefit and existing institutional requirements.

**Evaluation:** required deadlines met, outstanding findings, corrected records, controlled curricula, valid handler/K9 authorizations, and verified graduate outcomes.

## 2. Education Goals

### Objective 1 — Conduct Market Research and Update Marketing Plan

**2026 goal:** Increase qualified inquiry and application activity and recruit students who can realistically succeed in the chosen AK9I programs. The former 15% inquiry-growth goal requires a fresh baseline and executive approval.

**Responsible functions:** Marketing and Business Development, with Student Services, School Administration, and Data/Systems.

**Area 1.1 — Market research**
- Evaluate veterans, transitioning service members, civilians seeking a career change, law-enforcement personnel, and employer-sponsored students against program fit and requirements.
- Analyze competing programs, market geography, training duration, tuition/funding, career expectations, and historical inquiries.
- Interview applicants, alumni, employers, and instructors and distinguish representative evidence from anecdotes.

**Area 1.2 — Marketing plan**
- Use AK9I's 2026 Brand Standards and accurate qualifications, outcomes, and training descriptions.
- Develop clear audience-specific messaging and source-tracked landing or inquiry paths.
- Allocate resources according to qualified lead, completed application, actual start, and financial contribution.

**Area 1.3 — Campaigns**
- Coordinate relevant social/search outreach, veteran and military events, campus tours, career webinars, professional demonstrations, and alumni referrals.
- Record originating channel and source in Salesforce and assign a named owner and next action.
- Test content, destination pages, qualification rules, and human follow-up before expanding paid budgets.

**Evaluation:** qualified inquiries, application rate, program-appropriate starts, source attribution, cost per student start, and marketing return.

### Objective 2 — Implement Targeted Marketing Strategies and Enrollment Initiatives

**2026 goal:** Use Salesforce and coordinated staff communications to support applicants from first engagement to confirmed start, successful training, measurable outcomes, enduring relationships, and advocacy. Growth targets will follow reconciliation of 2026 cohort data and capacity.

**Responsible functions:** Student Services, Marketing/BD, School Administration, Training, Data/Systems.

#### Area 2.1 — Targeted enrollment campaigns

- Segment audiences by program, eligibility, timing, source, benefit/funding path, and career intent.
- Provide realistic guidance on training requirements, approved credentials, expected outcomes, and next steps.
- Use guided webinars, consultations, student and employer proof, and measured follow-up.
- Differentiate Salesforce Lead/Contact status from the **seven-stage customer lifecycle**, which spans much more than a conventional sales opportunity.

#### Area 2.2 — Salesforce CRM, the seven-stage AK9I Customer Pipeline, and communications

**Canonical stages:** **Awareness → Consideration → Enrollment / Commitment → Training & Experience / Delivery → Loyalty → Recurring Relationships → Advocacy**.

The stages are consistent with the AK9I Operating System repository. **Outcomes and employment placement are managed within Stage 5 (Loyalty)**; they are not a competing eighth stage. **Recurring Relationships is Stage 6** and **Advocacy is Stage 7**.

| Stage | Primary purpose and transition | Communications and follow-up | Responsible records and teams |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. | **2. Advocacy** | Invite qualified, willing alumni, clients, and employees to represent AK9I credibly. | Testimonial/photo/video consent; referral and approved review invitations; alumni speaker and mentor opportunities; employer introductions; contract-client reference and case-study invitations. | Marketing/BD with alumni and account owners; record explicit permissions and attributable referrals. |

**Required process design**

1. For each stage, document **entry trigger, exit rule, accountable owner, approved messaging, timing, response SLA, exception, and next action**.
2. Configure messaging as **stage-specific sequences**, not undifferentiated mass campaigns. Pilot automated timing and confirm actual system licensing/capabilities before making deployment claims.
3. Use proposed pre-start touchpoints upon acceptance/commitment and, when lead time allows, approximately **30, 14, 7, 3, and 1 day(s) before course start**. Include missing documentation, payment readiness, instructional expectations, orientation, and housing information as appropriate.
4. Convert the Student Services **“red list”** into visible task prioritization and escalation; record contact attempts and outcomes rather than simply marking a student “missing.”
5. Issue a human follow-up when an accepted student remains unresponsive, has unresolved VA/funding questions, is missing critical records, or may defer.
6. Complete instructional records, certification data, housing checkout and graduation preparation before the final graduation activity. At graduation, secure alumni contact preference and the next employment-follow-up date.
7. Build alumni outcome contact processes that meet actual reporting/evidence requirements; where appropriate, use approximately 30/60/90-day check-ins and subsequent cohort follow-up.
8. Track separate choices for **informational SMS, marketing SMS, and no texts**, maintain consent evidence, and honor opt-outs and applicable law before enabling sequences.
9. End or reroute sequences at withdrawal, changed start date, opt-out, actual course start, graduation, account reassignment, or other relevant stage changes.
10. Give every active Salesforce record an owner, next action, due date, and source; audit response times, aging, conversions, no-contact outcomes, and sequence performance.
11. Establish a **parallel contract-client lane** under the same seven stages: Awareness → Consideration → Commitment/Award → Service Delivery → Loyalty → Recurring Relationships → Advocacy.

**Salesforce minimum record fields:** customer lane; person/organization; stage; source/campaign; assigned owner; next action/date; interest/program; necessary application/benefit readiness; communication consent/preferences; monetary milestones; actual start; completion/outcome; continuing relationship and referral/advocacy status.

#### Area 2.3 — Student support services

- Maintain the Student Resource Packet and related block of instruction reported in Paul's notes, including controlled versions, accurate career/startup guidance, and responsible staff.
- Provide approved advising, student-benefit assistance, instructor introductions, orientation, career support, and contact resolution.
- Offer guided information sessions and tours when valuable; the original fixed twice-monthly open-house commitment is removed.
- Maintain clear instructions for a graduate starting a detection-dog business, including direct self-registration pathways, licensing research, and optional professional support.

#### Area 2.4 — Enrollment evaluation and attribution

Paul's markup reports approximately **a 50% decline in fourth-quarter enrollment** and later student volume of approximately **7–10 per month**. His proposed explanation refers to an economic downturn. Neither the comparison year, denominator, nor cause has yet been verified; the original notes are not sufficient to establish a causal diagnosis.

**Required investigation**

1. Specify the quarter and year, comparison period, program, location, and whether “enrollment” means application, acceptance, paid commitment, or first-day actual start.
2. Reconcile Salesforce entries, historic rosters, tuition/VA records, and financial data. Compare matching quarters across multiple years and by course length.
3. Analyze funnel movement: inquiries → qualified leads → applications → accepted → paid/committed → first-day starts. Examine quantity, conversion, aging, withdrawals, and funding constraints at each step.
4. Test competing internal explanations: responsiveness, missed follow-up, changes in ads/events/referrals, pricing, benefit documentation, calendar availability, instructor capacity, website friction, and rival programs.
5. Test economic explanations with **time-matched, geographically and audience-relevant external indicators** instead of assuming an economy-wide downturn explains AK9I's own variance.
6. Contact a suitable sample of deferrals/withdrawals for reason coding; distinguish life circumstances, inability to pay, unresolved benefits, timing, disqualification, expectations mismatch, and no response.
7. Publish an evidence matrix marking each explanation **supported, plausible but unproven, or contradicted**; only assign causal attribution when enough data support it.
8. Approve targeted interventions and measure whether start rate improves.

**Evaluation:** source-to-start conversion, application and acceptance rates, contact speed, benefit/document completeness, deferrals and no-shows with reason codes, actual starts, and confidence in the supported causes.
