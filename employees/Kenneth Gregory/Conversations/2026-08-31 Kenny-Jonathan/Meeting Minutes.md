# Meeting Minutes — Kenneth Gregory & Jonathan Holman

**Date:** August 31, 2026  
**Organization:** AK9I  
**Participants:** Kenneth "Kenny" Gregory, Jonathan Holman  
**Purpose:** Employee role-alignment interview focused on Training leadership, curriculum, student experience, culture, management systems, marketing, and organizational scalability.

## Discussion Summary

### 1. Kenny's current operating role
Kenny described a role extending beyond normal instructor duties. His work includes law-enforcement/federal-student training, operational K-9 instruction, remediation of difficult dogs, troubleshooting instructor/student problems, student welfare and incident response, and informal leadership across the Training function.

He described becoming a frequent "catch-all" resource because students and staff seek him out when normal processes do not resolve an issue.

### 2. Training leadership and role clarity
The discussion identified a need to define the Training leadership seat independently of the individual currently occupying it. Kenny's preferred operating model is less about being permanently attached to one class and more about moving across classes, coaching instructors, providing advanced operational input, teaching key orientation/foundation content, and intervening when students or dogs need specialized help.

### 3. Curriculum review
Kenny expressed concern that portions of the curriculum appear outdated, overly derivative, or insufficiently tied to how staff actually train today. He recommended a line-by-line review rather than isolated edits.

Specific concerns included whether AK9I should present certain apprehension/bite-work content when liability, student employability, and actual staff capability may not support it, and whether titles such as "master trainer" accurately reflect what a short program can legitimately produce.

### 4. Student expectations, kennel work, and culture
Kenny supported making canine care, kennel sanitation, discipline, and related responsibilities a clearly defined part of the training experience rather than an informal extra duty. The expectation should be communicated before or at entry so students understand that responsible working-dog care is part of professional development.

The group also discussed making better use of the residential-campus environment through structured learning, coaching, and canine-handling opportunities outside the narrow classroom block without turning students into replacement labor.

### 5. Management operating system
Jonathan outlined the AK9I Management Operating System concept: clear ownership, management cadence, KPIs, documented follow-up, and defined reward/discipline mechanisms. Kenny supported the need for consistent management expectations rather than informal or personality-driven oversight.

### 6. Brand and culture
The discussion connected Training leadership directly to the AK9I brand. Kenny emphasized that instructor behavior, appearance, credibility, and operational competence shape how students perceive the company. The goal is to institutionalize that credibility so the AK9I brand does not depend on one instructor's personal reputation.

### 7. Customer pipeline, alumni, and placement
The conversation addressed the need to improve pre-enrollment fit, reduce attrition before students arrive, make graduation meaningful, and convert graduates into advocates. Kenny noted that he has personally carried significant placement and alumni-support activity; the discussion concluded that these capabilities should become organizational processes rather than remain person-dependent.

### 8. Pre-enrollment screening and employability
Kenny raised the need for more deliberate screening of prospective students when known circumstances could make them effectively unemployable in the working-dog field. The purpose is not to exclude people arbitrarily, but to avoid selling a career outcome that AK9I reasonably knows may not be available to the student.

### 9. Marketing and media
Kenny argued that AK9I's marketing needs to reflect how younger audiences consume content. Short-form video and platforms such as Instagram, TikTok, and similar channels should play a larger role rather than relying primarily on Facebook-style static content.

Marketing should also accurately portray the program so AK9I attracts students who want the training AK9I actually provides.

### 10. Scale and future-state Training structure
The discussion framed current role design as a scalability problem. If AK9I grows to multiple instructors, campuses, or states, the Training function needs defined seats, standards, reporting, and repeatable methods that can be reproduced without depending on one person's informal influence.

### 11. Investment interest
Kenny confirmed continued interest in the business as a potential investor, subject to clear governance, ownership terms, decision rights, and confidence that the operating model is being stabilized. No investment decision was made in this conversation.

## Decisions / Conclusions

- Define the Training leadership seat based on required outcomes and scalability rather than historical title alone.
- Conduct a detailed curriculum review, including liability-sensitive and employability-sensitive content.
- Make professional canine care, kennel standards, and student responsibility explicit parts of the training model where appropriate.
- Institutionalize placement, alumni support, and instructor quality so they are AK9I capabilities rather than person-dependent activities.
- Use management cadence and measurable expectations to improve instructor accountability and recognition.
- Modernize marketing toward short-form, audience-appropriate content that accurately reflects AK9I's program.
